PokéLeet

Privacy Policy

Last updated November 2, 2026

PokéLeet is a free, open-source fan project by Sharon Chen. This page explains what the PokéLeet website collects when you use it, why, and what you can do about it. The short version: only what it takes to run your account, nothing for advertising, and you can delete it all yourself at any time.

What PokéLeet collects

  • Your account: your email address. If you sign in with Google, your Google account’s email address and ID. Passwords are handled by Amazon Cognito; PokéLeet never sees them.
  • What you add to the app: your problems (titles, links, difficulty, tags and solution code), your trainer name and optional photo, and your game progress (Pokémon, items, quests and study-session totals).
  • Error logs: when the server runs into a problem, it logs what went wrong, which can include your account ID. These logs are deleted after two weeks.

PokéLeet doesn’t use analytics, advertising or tracking cookies, and doesn’t sell or share your information.

How it’s used

Only to run PokéLeet: to sign you in, to save your problems and progress and show them on any device you sign in on, and to email you sign-up and password-reset codes.

Where it’s stored, and who else is involved

  • Amazon Web Services hosts PokéLeet in the United States (us-east-1): sign-in (Cognito, which also sends the email codes), your data (DynamoDB and Aurora PostgreSQL) and the website itself.
  • Google, only if you choose to sign in with Google.
  • LeetCode: when you look up a problem, PokéLeet’s server asks LeetCode for that problem’s details. Only the problem’s name is sent.
  • Google Fonts and pokemondb.net: your browser loads the fonts and Pokémon pictures from them, so like any website they see your IP address.

In your browser

PokéLeet keeps your sign-in in your browser’s local storage so you stay signed in, along with any changes that haven’t reached the server yet. The demo keeps everything there and sends nothing.

The Tracker extension

The optional PokéLeet Tracker browser extension tells PokéLeet whether a LeetCode tab is the one you’re looking at, so study sessions only count time spent there. It doesn’t record or send your browsing history.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Your data is kept for as long as you have an account.
  • Delete your account at any time with Backups → Delete account on your trainer card (Dashboard). Your account and data are deleted right away; copies in backups kept for disaster recovery expire within 35 days.
  • Download your data with Backups → Export Backup.
  • You can also email the address below to ask about or delete your data.

Security

Everything travels over HTTPS and is encrypted at rest by AWS. In the app, only your sign-in can read or change your data, and access to the AWS account behind it is limited to PokéLeet’s maker.

Children

PokéLeet isn’t meant for children under 13, and doesn’t knowingly collect their information.
